01 THE PROBLEM

Voice control has an integration problem.

Embedded-product teams need to connect microphones, noise handling, wake-word detection, audio buffering, networking, speech recognition, intent parsing, and privacy controls.

Streaming everything to a server consumes bandwidth and exposes ambient audio. Running full speech recognition on the device can push hardware cost and power beyond a small product’s budget.

THE DESIGN QUESTIONHow can an affordable device respond to voice without continuously streaming audio?
02 THE PROPOSED SYSTEM

A clear boundary between hearing and sending.

The ESP32-S3 reference design keeps wake-word recognition on the device and makes networking event driven.

01◉MicrophoneCapture ambient sound
02≋Local front endClean audio + detect speech
03✳Wake wordDetect “Utho Kikkar”
04↗Command uploadSend after trigger
05⌁Speech + intentInterpret the request
5–10 second rolling buffer

Keeps recent audio available around the trigger so the beginning of a command is not lost.

Backend-agnostic handoff

Product teams can connect the triggered audio to a cloud or self-hosted speech service.

Privacy by architecture

Ambient audio stays on the device before activation. Backend data handling still needs its own review.

04 COST & MARKET CONTEXT

A small-board starting point.

The reference platform uses an ESP32-S3 with built-in Wi-Fi and local wake-word support. Its value is the reusable integration around the model: buffering, privacy gating, configuration, and a flexible speech-service handoff.
